The question

How much thermal-zoning and floor-stacking detail does a building energy model need?

Urban-scale models simplify buildings: rooms merge into zones, storeys are represented by multipliers or merged into a single zone. We compare nine such simplifications with a detailed EnergyPlus reference in which every room is a zone and every storey is explicit, and measure the error per reference conditioned floor area: hourly RMSE (W/m²) and annual demand (kWh/m²a).

Key findings

What we found

≤ 0.15 W/m²

Semantic merge is closest everywhere

Merging neighbouring rooms of the same type (SM) is the closest model in every building type, with a median hourly RMSE of at most 0.15 W/m².

76 % / 55 %

Often the single best model

SM is the sole best model in 76 % of contexts for cooling and 55 % for heating. Models that share one simulation count once, ties within 0.008 W/m², over the 974 contexts where SM differs from the reference.

1.2–1.9 W/m²

Building type matters

In the medium office, one zone per floor or one zone per building reaches a median hourly RMSE of 1.2–1.9 W/m².

0.3–10 %

Lost in aggregation

Median annual departures are only 0.3–10 % of demand, while median hourly RMSE spans 0.04–0.91 W/m² across models: annual totals hide the hourly error.

Hot → Cold

Climate shifts the error

Heating error grows from hot to cold climates. Cooling error peaks in hot climates for apartments, but in cold ones for the large office.

4–5×

Cost differs by program

In midrise apartments SM costs about the CPU time of the floor multiplier (SFZM); in the large office SFZM is 4–5× cheaper.

Methods

What is simplified

Six zonings simplify each storey's plan; three stackings simplify the storeys. Every model keeps the programs of the rooms it merges, so only geometry and zoning change.

Zoning, within a storey

  • SMSemantic merge: neighbouring rooms of the same type merge
  • SMJPSemantic merge with joined pieces: same-type zones also join across the plan
  • CMConditioned merge: rooms merge by conditioning flag
  • PC4.57Perimeter–core zoning with 4.57 m perimeter zones
  • PC3.0Perimeter–core zoning with 3.0 m perimeter zones
  • SZFOne zone per floor

Stacking, across storeys

  • SFZMFloor multiplier: representative storeys with multipliers, adiabatic interstorey surfaces
  • SZFTOne zone per floor entry
  • SZBOne zone per building

Reference (NS): every room a zone, every storey explicit.
